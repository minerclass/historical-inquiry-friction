Judge the choice with what could have been known.

Each room is one moment in the past, and someone in it faces a hard choice. Eight papers are in the room. Some belong to that moment. Others carry knowledge, words, or verdicts from later, and nothing on them says so.

Carry three papers that could have been held there to the table. Then, as a historian, appraise the choice.

You have a hindsight lantern. It shows at once whether a paper belongs, and every use adds presentist drift. Reading closely costs nothing.
